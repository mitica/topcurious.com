### 1. Alert, Canada

![Alert, Canada](/_images/f/f356911c8f36e52023ff7fb426e535a5-medium.webp)

Sits at 82 degrees north latitude. Functions mainly as a Canadian military and weather outpost. Supports fewer than 100 people year round. Remains cut off from roads and regular supplies for months.

### 2. Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard

![Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard](/_images/4/4fdd4759873f63eb2159d18fb220a42e-medium.webp)

World's northernmost permanently inhabited settlement. Hosts international polar research stations. Limits access to scientists and staff only. Experiences continuous daylight for four months each summer.

### 3. Longyearbyen, Svalbard

![Longyearbyen, Svalbard](/_images/7/7e6bddcf7ecc4ac8896e258f2fd806e8-medium.webp)

Largest town in Svalbard at 78 degrees north. Requires residents to carry rifles outside town limits. Bans burials due to permafrost conditions. Operates under unique Norwegian sovereignty rules.

### 4. Resolute, Canada

![Resolute, Canada](/_images/5/56e47c9b5422f17276d03823507fd871-medium.webp)

Small Inuit community on Cornwallis Island. Serves as a key hub for Arctic research flights. Records average winter temperatures below minus 30 Celsius. Maintains a population under 200 people.

### 5. Nord, Greenland

![Nord, Greenland](/_images/4/4b65405d5892b03e8c1508b71743e75c-medium.webp)

Danish military and weather station at 81 degrees north. Operates with rotating staff of about 20 people. Receives supply flights only a few times per year. Located on an ice-free peninsula surrounded by glaciers.

### 6. Barentsburg, Svalbard

![Barentsburg, Svalbard](/_images/e/e5eec0a2c9b10bcc08cebed1f90f7713-medium.webp)

Russian coal mining settlement at 78 degrees north. Features Soviet-era architecture and a small population. Maintains its own school and cultural facilities. Relies on coal exports and Russian government support.
